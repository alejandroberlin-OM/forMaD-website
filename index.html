How it works

Three steps. Seconds, not hours.

Step 01
Create your profile

Set up your profile once: name, CPSO number, hospital, contact details. Every form auto-fills the physician's Section 1 from that moment forward. Staff who support you can be added as delegates to prepare forms on your behalf.

Delegates for your staff
Step 02
Add context. Paste your notes.

Tell forMaD the key context and goal for the form in a few words, then paste your clinical notes. No patient name. No date of birth. No health card number. Just the clinical story: diagnosis, disease state, prior treatments, rationale. forMaD knows which evidence to pull, which insurer criteria apply, and what makes this case compelling to reviewers.

Built for Canadian health privacy law
Step 03
Review, verify, download.

forMaD generates a complete, evidence-based form with real trial citations, specific stopping criteria, and proper dosing references. You review every field. You add the patient details at the very end. You sign. The form is yours. We handled the writing.

Physician reviews and signs everything
Built around one rule

The AI never learns who your patient is.

This is not a compliance add-on. It is the design. Patient names, dates of birth, and health card numbers are added at the very end, used only to fill your PDF, and never sent to the AI. The AI gets the clinical story. You keep the identity.

De-identified by design You paste clinical context, not patient records. Diagnosis, disease state, treatment history. forMaD is designed so that is all the AI sees.
Your data stays private Built for Canadian health privacy law: our servers and database are in Canada, and our AI provider keeps nothing after replying, apart from narrow legal and safety exceptions. Your notes are never used to train any AI model.
Notes are never stored Notes are never stored. Drafts you choose to save are deleted 45 days after you last save or finish them.
You sign everything forMaD drafts. You review. You add the patient details at the final step. You submit. The clinical judgment is always yours.
